RESEARCH

Research areas

Planet Formation and Machine Learning

The discovery of multiple giant exoplanetary systems hosting terrestrial planets within their habitable zones motivates a systematic study of the formation of such systems using N-body simulations. Our approach begins by determining the parameters of stable orbits for systems of several giant planets with different masses, followed by simulations of terrestrial planet formation in the inner regions of the system, including hydrodynamical modeling where appropriate. The orbital stability of a planetary system depends strongly on the initial orbital parameters of its giant planets. Machine learning (ML) can substantially reduce the computational cost of assessing dynamical stability compared with direct N-body simulations. In particular, a pre-trained ML model can predict the stability of a system with an accuracy exceeding 93% in sub-seconds, providing a fast alternative for screening large numbers of planetary configurations.

Search for Missing Baryons through Scintillation

According to Big Bang nucleosynthesis and observations of the cosmic microwave background (CMB), baryonic matter (ordinary matter) accounts for about 4% of the total energy density of the Universe. However, observations suggest that roughly half of the expected baryonic matter is still unaccounted for on Galactic scales. One possible candidate for this missing baryonic matter is a population of primordial molecular hydrogen clouds. Although these clouds are transparent at optical wavelengths, turbulence within them can induce small-scale fluctuations in the refractive index, distorting the light from background stars and potentially causing observable scintillation for a terrestrial observer.
